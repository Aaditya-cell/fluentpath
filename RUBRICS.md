# Feedback rubrics

These rubrics are for formative practice. They are not official IELTS or PTE scoring rubrics and must not be presented as official scores.

## Writing

Give concise qualitative observations in four dimensions:

- **Task response:** addresses the prompt, covers required points, supports claims with relevant examples.
- **Organization:** clear progression, paragraphing, useful linking, readable sentence relationships.
- **Vocabulary:** appropriate range and word choice; note repeated or unclear wording only when present.
- **Grammar:** sentence variety, clarity, and recurring errors that affect meaning.

## Speaking

Give concise qualitative observations in six dimensions, using audio evidence only:

- **Task response:** answers the prompt and includes relevant details.
- **Organization:** ideas follow a clear order.
- **Vocabulary:** word choice is understandable and appropriate.
- **Grammar:** spoken sentence structures are clear; avoid penalizing informal speech without a reason.
- **Fluency:** pace, pauses, and continuity, based only on the clip.
- **Pronunciation:** comment only on audible intelligibility or specific sounds; do not infer a score from accent.

## Reading and Listening

Use the authored answer key for objective items. Show the selected/correct answer and a short explanation tied to the passage or audio script. Never use an LLM for these checks.

## Calibration before claiming estimated scores

- Ask qualified exam educators to review the rubrics and sample content.
- Collect consented learner examples and ratings from at least two raters for a subset.
- Hold back examples from prompt development to evaluate feedback quality.
- Compare AI comments with human ratings, document disagreement, revise, and repeat.
- Until evidence supports score estimates, show qualitative feedback only.
